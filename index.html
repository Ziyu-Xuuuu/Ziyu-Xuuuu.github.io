About Me

I am a senior undergraduate student jointly enrolled at the University of Michigan (GPA: 3.81/4.0) and Shanghai Jiao Tong University (GPA: 3.63/4.0). I view myself as a human-centered, systems-oriented translator of technology, bridging disciplines to advance control and autonomy for real-world robotic systems.

Currently, I work as a research assistant in two laboratories at the University of Michigan: the Curly Lab (Prof. Maani Ghaffari), where I develop perception and control systems for autonomous surface vehicles, and the IRAL Lab (Prof. Vasileios Tzoumas), where I work on resource-aware multi-drone coordination and active SLAM. I am also participating in a startup program, building interactive robotic systems that explore the playful, human-centered side of autonomy.

My research focuses on connecting abstract control theory with feasible robotic systems. I specialize in bridging classical control methods (MPC, Lie-MPC, nonlinear control) with modern deep learning approaches (vision transformers, multimodal perception) to build robots that operate reliably outside the lab. Beyond technical performance, I measure success by whether systems are understandable, trustworthy, and usable by people from diverse backgrounds—a perspective shaped by cross-disciplinary collaborations in robotics, quantitative finance, and interactive game development.
